Condensed, player-facing summaries of Core Rulebook Chapter 7 (printed pp. 151-196), one entry per named subject. Reading copy of `data/lore.json`, which is the version the app uses.

- **Tags:** Character, Event, Place, Faction, Item, Note. An entry may have several.

## Overview

### The Nine Ages
*note* · PDF p. 152 · `nine-ages`

Thedas counts nine ages of 100 years each, dated as age:year. Divine (Second Blight); Glory (Caedmon, first king of Ferelden; Exalted March on the Dales); Towers (Third Blight; Flemeth and the Korcari witches); Black (war that drove the werewolves extinct; first Orlesian invasion of Ferelden fails); Exalted (Fourth Blight; Calenhad; Grey Wardens settle in Ferelden); Steel (Avvar invade Ferelden); Storm (Wardens expelled from Ferelden; Exalted Marches against the Qunari); Blessed (second Orlesian invasion; civil war between Meghren and the Rebel Queen); Dragon (Maric takes the throne; a dragon appears).

See also: The Second Blight, Teyrn Caedmon, The Third Blight, Flemeth and the Witches of the Wilds, Werewolves, The Fourth Blight, King Calenhad, Grey Wardens, The Avvar, The Qunari Wars, Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden, King Maric, King Meghren, Moira, the Rebel Queen, The Exalted March on the Dales

### The Thedosian Calendar
*note* · PDF p. 158 · `thedosian-calendar`

Twelve months of 30 days plus five annums (annual holidays) that mark the seasons and the first day of the year. Low month names used in Ferelden: Wintermarch, Guardian, Drakonis, Cloudreach, Bloomingtide, Justinian, Solace, August, Kingsway, Harvestmere, Firstfall, Haring. Annums: First Day, Wintersend, Summerday, Funalis, Satinalia. The calendar dates back to early Tevinter; the Divine Age began a new count in 1195 TE.

See also: Divine Justinia I

### Dragon Age Canon and Campaigns
*note* · PDF p. 154 · `canon-and-campaigns`

The chapter is a jumping-off point, not a full setting guide, and avoids spoiling other Dragon Age stories. Tables are free to deviate from canon, for example when player characters defeat figures who live on in published lore.

### Thedas (Overview)
*place* · PDF p. 153 · `thedas`

A land of uneasy neighbors and shifting, locally understood borders. The chapter focuses on Orlais, Tevinter and Ferelden, with extra detail on elves and dwarves.

See also: Tevinter Imperium, Orlais, Ferelden, The Dales, Orzammar

## Ferelden

### Ferelden
*place, faction* · PDF p. 153 · `ferelden`

Wide, barbarous country in the southeast, settled by the Alamarri's martial, honor-bound descendants. Temperate and fertile with harsh winters; capital Denerim; Orlais lies beyond the Frostbacks and the Free Marches across the Waking Sea.

See also: The Alamarri, Denerim, Frostback Mountains, Orlais, The Free Marches, The Waking Sea, The Bannorn, The Fereldan People

### The Alamarri
*faction* · PDF p. 153 · `alamarri`

Warlike, animistic tribes led by banns who revered the wolf and paired young warriors with cubs. Ancestors of the Fereldans, and the parent people of the Avvar and Chasind. Alone in Thedas they refused Tevinter rule.

See also: The Avvar, The Chasind, Werewolves, Tevinter Imperium, Fereldan Nobility

### Werewolves
*note* · PDF p. 154 · `werewolves`

Legend: the first werewolf was a wolf who betrayed his master to evil gods for a man's shape. The tribes united to slaughter them and drove the survivors into the forests. The Black Age war drove them to extinction, though some folk say a few lurk still.

See also: The Alamarri, Brecilian Forest, The Nine Ages

### The Avvar
*faction* · PDF p. 154 · `avvar`

Alamarri who split off after a civil war and live high in the Frostbacks, keeping the old nature gods, refusing kings and despising lowlanders. Winter raids on Ferelden are quick strikes, 'like fighting the weather'. They invaded Ferelden in the Steel Age.

See also: The Alamarri, Frostback Mountains, Ferelden, Luthias Dwarfson, Morrighan'nan

### The Chasind
*faction* · PDF p. 154 · `chasind`

Wilders of the Korcari Wilds, descended from Alamarri who went south. Once invaded the north with 'white shadows' and conquered the valley tribes for a time; later produced Flemeth and her witch daughters.

See also: The Alamarri, The Korcari Wilds, Flemeth and the Witches of the Wilds, Cormac

### The Korcari Wilds
*place* · PDF p. 154 · `korcari-wilds`

Swampy forest and tundra in the far south of Ferelden, home of the Chasind. Borders the Hinterlands and Southron Hills; what lies beyond is unknown even to the Chasind.

See also: The Chasind, The Hinterlands, Southron Hills

### Flemeth and the Witches of the Wilds
*character* · PDF p. 154 · `flemeth`

Legendary witch who rose among the Chasind. She and her Korcari-witch daughters led armies against the north until Cormac's host burned the witches. A separate legend makes her a Highever noblewoman whose grief called a spirit of Vengeance.

See also: The Chasind, Cormac, Highever's Secession, The Korcari Wilds

### Cormac
*character* · PDF p. 154 · `cormac`

Hero who led Alamarri fighters and Orzammar dwarves against Flemeth's army. The Chasind were all but destroyed and the witches burned.

See also: Flemeth and the Witches of the Wilds, The Alamarri, Orzammar, The Chasind

### Tevinter Invasions of Ferelden
*event* · PDF p. 154 · `tevinter-invasions-of-ferelden`

Tevinter tried three times to conquer Ferelden and was beaten each time by the Alamarri, Avvar and Chasind acting together. The fourth push held the western valley long enough to build fortresses and the Imperial Highway, at ruinous cost. A posting there was seen as exile or a death sentence.

See also: Tevinter Imperium, The Alamarri, The Avvar, The Chasind, The Imperial Highway, Ostagar, The Bane of Ostagar

### Ostagar
*place* · PDF p. 154 · `ostagar`

Fortress at the south end of the Imperial Highway, built to watch for the Chasind, with mortar so magically reinforced that legend says nothing can fully tear it down. It fell to the Bane of Ostagar; King Cailan died here as the Fifth Blight began.

See also: The Imperial Highway, The Bane of Ostagar, The Chasind, King Cailan, The Fifth Blight

### The Bane of Ostagar
*character* · PDF p. 155 · `bane-of-ostagar`

Nameless barbarian hero who found a way to breach Ostagar, breaking Tevinter's will to stay. Avvar, Chasind and Fereldans all claim him.

See also: Ostagar, The Avvar, The Chasind, Tevinter Invasions of Ferelden

### The Imperial Highway
*place* · PDF p. 161 · `imperial-highway`

Tevinter road running from Minrathous to Ostagar; crumbled in places but still much used. In Ferelden it unofficially divides the Southron Hills from the Hinterlands.

See also: Minrathous, Ostagar, Southron Hills, The Hinterlands, Tevinter Imperium

### Hafter
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `hafter`

First teyrn of Ferelden. Brought some peace to the Alamarri and led them against the darkspawn in the Second Blight.

See also: Teyrn Caedmon, The Second Blight, The Alamarri

### Teyrn Caedmon
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `caedmon`

Hafter's grandson, the first warlord to try to be King of Ferelden. He failed, but made the Andrastian Chantry the official religion.

See also: Hafter, Chantry of Andraste

### King Brandel
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `brandel`

Waged a decades-long guerrilla war against the Orlesian usurpers and fell when Denerim was sacked.

See also: Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden, Denerim, Moira, the Rebel Queen

### Moira, the Rebel Queen
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `rebel-queen`

Brandel's daughter, passed over by the Orlesians. She spent her life leading the Fereldan resistance and was assassinated by arls in 8:96 Blessed; her son Maric survived.

See also: King Brandel, King Meghren, King Maric, Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden

### King Meghren
*character* · PDF p. 175 · `meghren`

Orlesian lord Meghren Dufayel, placed on the Fereldan throne by Emperor Florian (with Grand Cleric Bronarch's blessing). Rebels flocked to Moira instead.

See also: Moira, the Rebel Queen, Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden, The Chantry in Ferelden

### King Maric
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `maric`

Son of the Rebel Queen. With Loghain Mac Tir and Rowan Guerrin he rallied the rebels, won at River Dane in 8:99 and began a golden age. Made peace with Empress Celene and was later lost at sea.

See also: Moira, the Rebel Queen, Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir, Battle of River Dane, Empress Celene I, King Cailan

### Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir
*character* · PDF p. 158 · `loghain`

Commoner whose tactical brilliance made him Maric's favored general and Teyrn of Gwaren. His daughter Anora married Cailan; his betrayal of Cailan tarnished his legacy.

See also: King Maric, Queen Anora, King Cailan, Gwaren, The Fifth Blight

### Queen Anora
*character* · PDF p. 158 · `anora`

Loghain's daughter and Cailan's queen. She bore no heir, leaving the Theirin line without one.

See also: Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir, King Cailan

### King Cailan
*character* · PDF p. 156 · `cailan`

Maric's only son and king after Maric was lost at sea. He died at Ostagar at the start of the Fifth Blight without an heir, throwing Ferelden into chaos and straining the truce with Orlais.

See also: King Maric, Queen Anora, Ostagar, The Fifth Blight, Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir, Empress Celene I

### Battle of River Dane
*event* · PDF p. 175 · `battle-of-river-dane`

8:99 Blessed. A high dragon from the Frostbacks, which had terrorized the countryside for months, turned on the Orlesian army and broke it, handing Maric victory. The dragon heralded the Dragon Age.

See also: King Maric, Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden, King Meghren

### The Fereldan People
*note* · PDF p. 156 · `ferelden-people`

Fiercely independent; respect is earned, not inherited. No serf class, selling slaves is illegal, and courage and martial skill count for more than wealth or ancestry. They prize their word and hesitate to promise what they might not deliver. Foreigners find their arts crude but powerful.

See also: Ferelden, Fereldan Nobility

### Fereldan Nobility
*faction* · PDF p. 157 · `ferelden-nobility`

Nobles exist to fight for the people and do not own the land; freeholders pick their bann or arl each year. Ranks: King or Queen, teyrns, arls, banns, knights. Commoner soldiers of exceptional skill can be knighted.

See also: The Landsmeet, Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir, Denerim

### The Landsmeet
*faction* · PDF p. 158 · `landsmeet`

Legislative body of all Fereldan nobles; meets once a season in Denerim to vote on the King's Law and air grievances. Absent nobles send proxies.

See also: Fereldan Nobility, Denerim

### Fereldan Crafthouses
*faction* · PDF p. 158 · `crafthouses`

Semi-formal guilds born of Alamarri craftsmen who swore crafthouse before tribe. No formal political power, but total control of their crafts.

See also: Traders' Crafthouse, Ferelden

### Traders' Crafthouse
*faction* · PDF p. 159 · `traders-crafthouse`

Created when dwarven merchants began moving other crafthouses' wares. Now handles selling across Ferelden and as far as Orlais and the Free Marches.

See also: Fereldan Crafthouses, Surfacers, The Free Marches, Orlais

### Law in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 160 · `fereldan-law`

Few laws; arms, gambling and drink are unregulated. Royal arbiters called blackhallers hear disputes and local sheriffs keep the peace, releasing suspects on a bond. Punishment is quick: whipping, fines, disfigurement or execution.

See also: Denerim

### Living in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 160 · `living-in-ferelden`

Cities spread haphazardly from a central keep, rich inside and labyrinthine outside. Rural freeholds are communal farms run by family councils. Almost nothing but slaves is illegal to sell. Most Fereldans follow the Chantry.

See also: Chantry of Andraste

### Dogs and the Mabari
*note* · PDF p. 160 · `fereldan-dogs`

Dogs replaced wolves in Fereldan esteem, and communities 'own' them collectively. The famous mabari is a huge mastiff-like war hound that follows complex orders.

See also: The Alamarri

### Elves in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 159 · `fereldan-elves`

Mostly servants and laborers in alienages, but paid and with rights, unlike elsewhere. Many say they would rather be poor freemen than rich slaves.

See also: Alienages, The Dalish

### Dwarves in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 159 · `fereldan-dwarves`

Most are Surfacers, scorned by Orzammar but still trading with it. Their dealings with Fereldan crafters led to the Traders' Crafthouse.

See also: Surfacers, Traders' Crafthouse, Orzammar

### The Chantry in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 159 · `fereldan-chantry`

Honored but politically weak; priests count as part of the crafting class. Grand Cleric Bronarch's crowning of the Orlesian usurper Meghren hurt its standing.

See also: Chantry of Andraste, King Meghren

### Magic in Ferelden
*note* · PDF p. 159 · `fereldan-magic`

Fereldans are superstitious and distrustful of magic. Mages must join the Circle, whose tower sits on a remote island since a mob burned the Denerim one. Templars guard every Circle.

See also: Circle of Magi, Circle Tower, Templar Order, Apostates and Maleficarum, Denerim

### Apostates and Maleficarum
*note* · PDF p. 159 · `apostates-and-maleficarum`

Mages outside the Circle are apostates, hunted by templars. Those who practice forbidden blood magic are maleficarum and feared above all.

See also: Templar Order, Blood Magic in Tevinter, Circle of Magi

### Circle of Magi
*faction* · PDF p. 159 · `circle-of-magi`

Chantry institution that confines mages under templar watch. Orlais founded its Circles at the Nevarran Accord. Tevinter's Circle is far more permissive, and Circle mages helped beat the Qunari.

See also: Templar Order, Nevarran Accord, Circle Tower, The White Spire, Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine, The Qunari Wars

### Circle Tower
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `circle-tower`

Seat of Ferelden's Circle of Magi, on an island in northern Lake Calenhad and visible for miles.

See also: Lake Calenhad, Circle of Magi

### Templar Order
*faction* · PDF p. 159 · `templars`

Chantry knights who guard Circles and hunt apostates, with authority to destroy mages they judge dangerous. Born of the Inquisition at the Nevarran Accord; leadership sits in the White Spire. Retired, lyrium-dependent templars settle in Val Royeaux.

See also: Circle of Magi, Nevarran Accord, The White Spire, Val Royeaux, Lyrium, Seekers of Truth

### Nevarran Accord
*event* · PDF p. 169 · `nevarran-accord`

1:20 Divine. Drakon I brought the Inquisition under the Chantry as the Templar Order and the Divine's own Seekers of Truth, and the Circles of Magi were founded in Orlais.

See also: Emperor Kordillus Drakon I, Templar Order, Seekers of Truth, Circle of Magi

### Seekers of Truth
*faction* · PDF p. 169 · `seekers-of-truth`

The Divine's own arm of the Inquisition, formalized at the Nevarran Accord.

See also: Nevarran Accord, Templar Order, Chantry of Andraste

### Denerim
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `denerim`

Capital of Ferelden, in the east; seat of the court and Landsmeet. Opened to trade by Calenhad and sacked by the Orlesians.

See also: Ferelden, The Landsmeet, King Calenhad, Orlesian Occupation of Ferelden

### The Bannorn
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `bannorn`

Great central valley and farming heartland. Control is split among many banns, from the Alamarri word for chieftain.

See also: Ferelden, Fereldan Nobility

### Brecilian Forest
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `brecilian-forest`

Cursed wilderness in the east and southeast, said to hold thieves, murderers, werewolves and moving trees. Only the Dalish enter regularly.

See also: The Dalish, Werewolves, Gwaren

### Gwaren
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `gwaren`

Port city on the Amaranthine Ocean, home to those brave enough to live by the Brecilian Forest.

See also: Brecilian Forest, Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir, Amaranthine Ocean

### Amaranthine Ocean
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `amaranthine-ocean`

Vast, uncharted ocean forming Ferelden's eastern border.

See also: Gwaren

### Southron Hills
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `southron-hills`

Rocky highlands east of the Imperial Highway, reaching the Brecilian Forest and the Korcari Wilds.

See also: The Imperial Highway, Brecilian Forest, The Korcari Wilds

### The Hinterlands
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `hinterlands`

Land west of the Imperial Highway, from Lake Calenhad to the Frostback foothills, bordering the Korcari Wilds.

See also: The Imperial Highway, Lake Calenhad, Frostback Mountains, The Korcari Wilds

### Lake Calenhad
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `lake-calenhad`

Huge clear-blue lake fed by Frostback runoff and said to have mystical properties. Home of the Circle Tower.

See also: Circle Tower, Frostback Mountains

### Frostback Mountains
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `frostback-mountains`

Range on Ferelden's west, home to the Avvar, with ice bridges between peaks. Orzammar lies beneath its northern end; Orlais lies beyond.

See also: The Avvar, Orzammar, Orlais, Lake Calenhad

### The Waking Sea
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `waking-sea`

Separates Ferelden's Coastlands from the Free Marches. Val Royeaux sits on its inland coast and Kirkwall on its shore.

See also: The Free Marches, Val Royeaux, Kirkwall

### The Free Marches
*place* · PDF p. 156 · `free-marches`

Wealthy city-states controlling the center of the continent, north of the Waking Sea.

See also: The Waking Sea

## Blights and Darkspawn

### The First Blight
*event* · PDF p. 155 · `first-blight`

Magisters who stormed the Golden City were cast out as the first darkspawn and woke the Old God Dumat as an Archdemon in 800 TE. The Blight laid Tevinter waste for nearly two centuries, until Grey Wardens killed Dumat at the Battle of the Silent Plains.

See also: The Golden City, Darkspawn, Dumat, Battle of the Silent Plains, Tevinter Imperium, Grey Wardens

### Darkspawn
*faction* · PDF p. 155 · `darkspawn`

Chantry teaching: twisted ex-mages who crept into the deep places. Each Blight is a horde led by an Archdemon; the land withers, rivers foul and the sky turns ashen. Dwarves see the whole span since as one endless war.

See also: The Golden City, Dumat, The Deep Roads, The First Blight, Orzammar, Grey Wardens

### Dumat
*character* · PDF p. 155 · `dumat`

Old God, the Dragon of Silence. Taught blood magic to Archon Thalsian, was imprisoned underground by the Maker, then corrupted and woken by the first darkspawn to become the first Archdemon. Legend also blames him for erasing Barindur.

See also: The Old Gods, Archon Thalsian, Darkspawn, Barindur, The First Blight, Blood Magic in Tevinter

### Battle of the Silent Plains
*event* · PDF p. 163 · `battle-of-silent-plains`

Grey Wardens killed the Archdemon Dumat in southern Tevinter in -203 Ancient (992 TE), ending the First Blight about two centuries after it began.

See also: Dumat, Grey Wardens, The First Blight

### The Second Blight
*event* · PDF p. 169 · `second-blight`

The Archdemon Zazikel rose in 1:5 Divine. Drakon I marched north, won at Cumberland (1:16) and relieved the Grey Wardens at Weisshaupt (1:33); Zazikel fell at the Battle of Starkhaven. Tevinter abandoned the Anderfels and Minrathous was nearly sacked. Hafter fought darkspawn in Ferelden.

See also: Emperor Kordillus Drakon I, Cumberland, Weisshaupt Fortress, Grey Wardens, Emperor Kordillus Drakon II, Hafter, Tevinter Imperium

### The Third Blight
*event* · PDF p. 164 · `third-blight`

Arose in the Towers Age and forced the Imperial and Andrastian Chantries to turn from their feud. Afterwards Orlais and Tevinter grabbed land, and Orlais claimed the Nevarra it had saved.

See also: Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine, Chantry of Andraste, Orlais, Tevinter Imperium, Nevarra vs. Orlais

### The Fourth Blight
*event* · PDF p. 191 · `fourth-blight`

Struck in the Exalted Age and ended the Exalted Marches against the Imperial Chantry. In this age Calenhad united Ferelden and the Grey Wardens settled there.

See also: Exalted Marches, King Calenhad, Grey Wardens

### The Fifth Blight
*event* · PDF p. 158 · `fifth-blight`

Began at Ostagar, where King Cailan died. Warden Commander Duncan and companions defeated the Archdemon, and Ferelden was restored under a new ruler.

See also: Ostagar, King Cailan, Warden Commander Duncan, Grey Wardens, Teyrn Loghain Mac Tir

### Grey Wardens
*faction* · PDF p. 155 · `grey-wardens`

Order that slew Dumat at the Silent Plains. They took a permanent post in Ferelden in the Exalted Age and were expelled in the Storm Age. After Drakon relieved Weisshaupt they converted en masse to the Andrastian faith. A dwarf loses no caste by joining.

See also: Battle of the Silent Plains, Weisshaupt Fortress, Emperor Kordillus Drakon I, Chantry of Andraste, Paragon Moroc the Maul, The Dwarven Caste System, The Fifth Blight

### Warden Commander Duncan
*character* · PDF p. 158 · `duncan`

Famed Warden Commander credited, with his companions, with defeating the Archdemon of the Fifth Blight.

See also: Grey Wardens, The Fifth Blight

### The Golden City
*place* · PDF p. 162 · `golden-city`

The Maker's city in the Fade. Chantry teaching: in 800 TE Tevinter magisters forced their way in and blackened it with their sin, and were cast out as the first darkspawn. The Maker then turned away from the world.

See also: The Fade (the Beyond), The Magisterium, Darkspawn, The Maker

### The Fade (the Beyond)
*place* · PDF p. 161 · `the-fade`

Spirit realm the elves called the Beyond. Elven scholars taught Tevinter mages to enter it asleep using lyrium; magisters mapped it and eventually stormed the Golden City. Experiments at Aeonar left the Veil thin.

See also: Lyrium, The Golden City, Aeonar, The Magisterium, Uthenera

## Tevinter Imperium

### Tevinter Imperium
*place, faction* · PDF p. 161 · `tevinter-imperium`

Ancient mage-ruled empire whose history touches all of Thedas. A Minrathous-based kingdom unified under Archon Darinius in 0 TE, it conquered most of the continent, then shrank through the Blights, rebellions and the Qunari wars. Today it is decaying, built on slavery and intrigue, and ruled openly by magisters again. Deceit is expected, and its politics resemble Orlais's Game without the finesse.

See also: Minrathous, Archon Darinius, The Magisterium, The First Blight, Slavery in Tevinter, Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine, The Qunari Wars, Orlais, Andraste

### Minrathous
*place* · PDF p. 161 · `minrathous`

Tevinter's capital on the Nocen Sea. Stone spires lift the magisters above the squalor, dwarven golems guard the gates, and no enemy has ever breached it. The Imperial Divine rules Chantry and Circle from the Argent Spire.

See also: Tevinter Imperium, Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine, Golems, The Imperial Highway

### The Magisterium
*faction* · PDF p. 162 · `magisterium`

Council of magisters formed by Darinius from the priesthood of the seven Old Gods. They rule cities, make law on magic, and were raised above the nobility as the Archon's court.

See also: Archon Darinius, The Old Gods, Archon's Signet

### The Old Gods
*faction* · PDF p. 162 · `old-gods`

Seven ancient dragons worshipped in Tevinter, said to have taught men magic. The Maker imprisoned them underground. Their temples were sacked when they stayed silent during the First Blight.

See also: Dumat, The Magisterium, The First Blight, Tevinter Imperium

### Archon Thalsian
*character* · PDF p. 161 · `thalsian`

Tevinter mage taught blood magic by Dumat. He built temples to the Old Gods and raised an army of blood mages.

See also: Dumat, Blood Magic in Tevinter, The Old Gods

### Archon Darinius
*character* · PDF p. 162 · `darinius`

First official Archon (-1195 Ancient, 0 TE). Formed the Magisterium, made magisters royalty, conquered the elves, sank Arlathan and allied with the dwarves under Endrin Stonehammer. His ferryman vision became the Archons' symbol.

See also: The Magisterium, Archon's Signet, Arlathan, Endrin Stonehammer, Kal-Sharok, Tevinter Imperium

### Archon's Signet
*item* · PDF p. 166 · `archon-signet`

Ring stamped with a hooded ferryman whose face beneath is the Archon's own, and the Archon's name below. Crushed before the magisters when an Archon dies.

See also: Archon Darinius, The Magisterium

### Blood Magic in Tevinter
*note* · PDF p. 161 · `blood-magic`

Dumat taught that fresh living blood boosts a mage far beyond lyrium. Blood magic is now outlawed in Tevinter as a political measure but still practiced, notably dream-seeing to manipulate others. Even the Imperial Chantry forbids using it to control minds.

See also: Dumat, Archon Thalsian, Apostates and Maleficarum, Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine

### Lyrium
*item* · PDF p. 161 · `lyrium`

Mineral elven scholars used to teach Tevinter mages to enter the Fade. Magisters infuse it into slaves, dwarves record the Memories in it, templars become dependent on it, and its trade drives Orzammar's wealth.

See also: The Fade (the Beyond), The Memories, Templar Order, Orzammar, Miner Caste

### Aeonar
*place* · PDF p. 166 · `aeonar`

Prison in northern Ferelden known only to the templars. Tevinter Fade experiments there thinned the Veil; Andraste's followers massacred the sleeping mages inside. Now a Chantry prison for apostates and maleficarum.

See also: The Fade (the Beyond), Templar Order, Andraste, Apostates and Maleficarum

### Kirkwall
*place* · PDF p. 166 · `kirkwall`

Originally Emerius, a Tevinter slave port on the Waking Sea where over a million slaves passed through the Gallows. Its streets were laid in magical shapes for blood rituals, and carved Old God faces glower from nearby black cliffs. Sir Michel Lafaille freed it from Qunari occupation in 7:60 Storm.

See also: The Waking Sea, Sir Michel Lafaille, The Old Gods, Slavery in Tevinter, The Qunari Wars

### Archon Nomaran
*character* · PDF p. 166 · `archon-nomaran`

Elected from the First Enchanters in 7:34 Storm. He repealed laws keeping mages out of politics, and within a century the magisters ruled openly again.

See also: The Magisterium, Tevinter Imperium

### Slavery in Tevinter
*note* · PDF p. 168 · `slavery-in-tevinter`

The Imperium runs on slavery. Slavers snatch travelers and elves from foreign docks while neighbors look away. Today's slaves die as fodder against the Qunari rather than in blood rituals.

See also: Tevinter Imperium, The Qunari Wars, Alienages

### Barindur
*place* · PDF p. 165 · `barindur`

Alamarri warlord and Andraste's husband, who united the tribes and marched on Tevinter. The Chantry says jealousy drove him to bargain her away to Archon Hessarian; history suggests he feared Tevinter's counterattack. The lands south of the Waking Sea were granted to his people.

### Battle of Valarian Fields
*event* · PDF p. 156 · `battle-of-valarian-fields`

Reduced to a central district and a few border thaigs. Respectable dwarves ignore the surface and leave casteless to handle trade. Shapers read omens of a new Archdemon, and some call these the final days.

### Dwarven Pride and Tradition

### Paragons
*faction* · PDF p. 183 · `paragons`

Exemplars above caste whose words carry a king's weight. Elevation requires an unprecedented feat and the Assembly's agreement, and creates a new noble house.

Eight castes: Noble, Warrior, Smith, Artisan, Miner, Merchant, Servant and Surface, with the casteless below. Caste follows the same-sex parent and is almost impossible to change except by becoming casteless. 'Noble hunters' seek to bear noble sons.

Sacred duels in the Proving Grounds that settle house conflicts, Assembly debates and honor challenges; ancestors are believed to bless the victor. Now also public entertainment. The casteless may not watch.

### Stonehammer Hall

Main route to Gherlen's Pass, lined with statues of the Paragons. Those leaving Orzammar must pass beneath their gaze.

### The Diamond Quarter

Highest district, with noble halls, the Royal Palace, the Assembly Chamber and the Shaperate, patrolled by elite Warrior Caste.

### The Commons

Central district where most dwarves live; higher tiers house higher castes. Merchants, surfacers included, trade on the lowest tier.

### Dust Town

Crumbling casteless district with no enforced law, home of the Carta.

### The Carta

Dwarven crime syndicate in Dust Town: lyrium smuggling, slave sales, prostitution and extortion.

## Religion and Belief

### Chantry of Andraste
*faction* · PDF p. 189 · `andrastian-chantry`

Dominant faith, built on Andraste's teachings in the Chant of Light and codified by Drakon I. Led by the Divine in Val Royeaux, it enforces limits on magic and calls Exalted Marches.

See also: Andraste, The Chant of Light, Emperor Kordillus Drakon I, Divine Justinia I, Exalted Marches, Imperial Chantry and the Black Divine, Templar Order

### The Chant of Light
*item* · PDF p. 190 · `chant-of-light`

Collected texts of the faith: history, moral teaching and hymns in canticles, stanzas and verses. Sung whole only at Val Royeaux. Holds that magic must serve mankind and that the Maker returns once it is sung in every corner of the world.

See also: Chantry of Andraste, Dissonant Verses, Andraste, Val Royeaux

### The Maker
*character* · PDF p. 189 · `maker`

The Chantry's creator. He turned from the world after the magisters' sin, returned for Andraste, and turned away again at her death.

See also: Andraste, The Golden City, The Chant of Light

### Exalted Marches
*event* · PDF p. 190 · `exalted-marches`

Chantry crusades. Andraste's war on Tevinter was the first, then the Dales, four against the Imperial Chantry, and three against the Qunari, ending at Llomerryn.

See also: Andraste, The Exalted March on the Dales, The Chantry Schism, The Qunari Wars, Llomerryn Accords

### The Stone and the Ancestors
*note* · PDF p. 192 · `the-stone-and-ancestors`

Dwarves, the Children of the Stone, have no gods. The worthy return to the Stone as guiding ancestors; the unworthy wander the Deep Roads as spirits. Ancestors speak through Provings.

See also: Provings, The Deep Roads, Legion of the Dead

### Legion of the Dead
*faction* · PDF p. 192 · `legion-of-the-dead`

Dishonored dwarves undergo a ceremonial death, are recorded as fallen heroes, and fight on the Deep Roads to restore their houses' honor.

See also: Dwarven Honor, The Deep Roads, Bownammar

### The Elven Pantheon
*faction* · PDF p. 192 · `elven-pantheon`

Gods once revered across Thedas, with months-long rituals. After Arlathan fell they fell silent. The Dalish divide them into the Creators and the Forgotten Ones.

See also: The Dalish, Arlathan, Elgar'nan, Mythal, Falon'Din, Dirthamen, Andruil, Sylaise, June, Ghilan'nain, Fen'Harel, The Forgotten Ones

### Elgar'nan
*character* · PDF p. 192 · `elgarnan`

Male head of the pantheon, patron of fatherhood and vengeance. He hurled his jealous father the sun from the sky.

See also: Mythal, The Elven Pantheon

### Mythal
*character* · PDF p. 193 · `mythal`

The Great Protector, patron of motherhood and justice. Calmed Elgar'nan and crafted the moon.

See also: Elgar'nan, The Elven Pantheon

### Falon'Din
*character* · PDF p. 193 · `falondin`

Friend of the Dead, god of death and fortune; guides souls beyond the Veil.

See also: Dirthamen, Elgar'nan, Mythal

### Dirthamen
*character* · PDF p. 193 · `dirthamen`

Keeper of Secrets, god of lore and Falon'Din's twin; master of Fear and Deceit.

See also: Falon'Din

### Andruil
*character* · PDF p. 193 · `andruil`

The Huntress, goddess of the hunt. Originator of the Vir Tanadahl.

See also: Vir Tanadahl (Way of Three Trees), Sylaise, Ghilan'nain

### Vir Tanadahl (Way of Three Trees)
*note* · PDF p. 194 · `vir-tanadahl`

Vir Assan, fly straight and do not waver; Vir Bor'Assan, bend but never break; Vir Adahlen, together we are stronger than the one. The Dalish add: we are the last of the elvhenan, and never again shall we submit.

See also: Andruil, The Dalish

### Sylaise
*character* · PDF p. 194 · `sylaise`

Hearthkeeper, goddess of the domestic arts; taught fire, herbs, weaving and healing.

See also: Andruil, June

### June
*character* · PDF p. 194 · `june`

Master of Crafts, who taught the elves to make bows and tools.

See also: Sylaise

### Ghilan'nain
*character* · PDF p. 194 · `ghilannain`

Mother of Halla, youngest of the gods; a mortal elf beloved of Andruil, transformed into a halla.

See also: Andruil

### Fen'Harel
*character* · PDF p. 194 · `fenharel`

The Dread Wolf, trickster beholden to both Creators and Forgotten Ones. His Betrayal sealed both away and left him alone with the world. The Dalish seek to appease him.

See also: The Forgotten Ones, The Elven Pantheon

### The Forgotten Ones
*faction* · PDF p. 194 · `forgotten-ones`

Enemy gods of terror, malevolence, spite and disease (Geldauran, Daern'thal, Anaris). Some fear secret cults still serve them.

See also: The Elven Pantheon, Fen'Harel

### The Qunari
*faction* · PDF p. 194 · `qunari`

People of the Qun; anyone who accepts it counts as Qunari. They have never abandoned their intent to bring the Qun to all, and use trade to study future foes and converts.

See also: The Qun, Par Vollen, The Qunari Wars, Koslun, Tamassrans, Ben-Hassrath, Tal-Vashoth, Saarebas

### The Qun
*note* · PDF p. 194 · `the-qun`

Philosophy and writings of Koslun that define everyone's role in Qunari society. Children are raised communally and assigned roles. Qunari find invisible gods absurd.

See also: Koslun, The Qunari, Tamassrans, Ben-Hassrath

### Koslun
*character* · PDF p. 194 · `koslun`

Ashkaari ('one who seeks') who wrote the Qun long ago.

See also: The Qun

### Tamassrans

Priestess-teachers who raise Qunari children and decide their roles.

See also: The Qun

### Ben-Hassrath

Enforcers of Qunari religious law, infamously brutal.

See also: The Qun, Tal-Vashoth

### Tal-Vashoth

Qunari who disavow the Qun and are exiled.

See also: Ben-Hassrath

### Saarebas
*note* · PDF p. 165 · `saarebas`

Qunari mages, kept under strict control and little used.

See also: The Qunari, Circle of Magi

### Par Vollen
*place* · PDF p. 165 · `par-vollen`

Qunari homeland island, site of the First Qunari War and the last Qunari holding after the Chantry pushes.

See also: The Qunari, The Qunari Wars

### Rivain and the Rivaini
*place, faction* · PDF p. 195 · `rivain`

Dark-skinned people forcibly converted to the Qun in the past, who keep a tribal faith of seers and hedge mages possessed by spirits. The Chant has never taken root there.

See also: The Qun, Antiva, The Qunari Wars

## Wars of Thedas

### Highever's Secession
*event* · PDF p. 196 · `highever-secession`

Bann Conobar's wife Flemeth fled with the poet Osen; Osen was slain and a vengeance spirit possessed her to kill Conobar. His captain Sarin Cousland then fought thirty years to secede Highever from Amaranthine.

See also: Flemeth and the Witches of the Wilds, Sarin Cousland, Highever, The Chasind, The Korcari Wilds

### Sarin Cousland
*character* · PDF p. 196 · `sarin-cousland`

Conobar's captain who became Bann of Highever. The Couslands rule there still.

See also: Highever's Secession, Highever

### Highever
*place* · PDF p. 196 · `highever`

Fereldan territory that won independence from Amaranthine, now ruled by the Couslands.

See also: Highever's Secession, Sarin Cousland

### King Calenhad
*character* · PDF p. 196 · `calenhad`

The Silver Knight, a commoner's son backed by Arl Myrddin. He united Ferelden, defeating Teyrn Simeon of Denerim in 5:42 Exalted with Circle mages and Ash Warriors, and was crowned by the Landsmeet. After a potion-aided seduction and Myrddin's death in a duel, he named his unborn son heir and vanished.

See also: Arl Myrddin, Queen Mairyn, Lady Shayna, Denerim, The Landsmeet, Circle of Magi, Emperor Kordillus Drakon I

### Arl Myrddin
*character* · PDF p. 196 · `arl-myrddin`

Powerful arl who backed Calenhad; his daughter Mairyn became queen. Killed by Calenhad in an honor duel.

See also: King Calenhad, Queen Mairyn

### Queen Mairyn
*character* · PDF p. 196 · `mairyn`

Calenhad's queen and Myrddin's daughter. Ruled until her son Weylan came of age.

See also: King Calenhad, Arl Myrddin

### Lady Shayna
*character* · PDF p. 196 · `lady-shayna`

Calenhad's closest friend, who secretly loved him; a witch persuaded her to seduce him with a potion.

See also: King Calenhad, Queen Mairyn

### Luthias Dwarfson
*character* · PDF p. 197 · `luthias-dwarfson`

Small-born Alamarri chieftain who wed Scaea, the Orzammar king's daughter. Seduced by Morrighan'nan, he fought her for fifteen years and died in their final battle.

See also: Morrighan'nan, Scaea, The Avvar, Orzammar

### Morrighan'nan
*character* · PDF p. 197 · `morrighannan`

Head of the Avvar who seduced Luthias; his refusal of her hand sparked a decade-long war. Revealed a warrior he killed was their son.

See also: Luthias Dwarfson, The Avvar

### Scaea
*character* · PDF p. 197 · `scaea`

Orzammar king's daughter and Luthias's bride. Left him when he betrayed her, but gifted him enchanted armor on the eve of the final battle.

See also: Luthias Dwarfson, Orzammar

### The Qunari Wars
*event* · PDF p. 165 · `qunari-wars`

In 6:30 Steel the Qunari landed, swept the north and reached Minrathous with cannon and blackpowder. Tevinter revolts and Circle mages drove them back; Exalted Marches followed in 7:25, 7:52 and 7:84. The Llomerryn Accords ended the war for everyone but Tevinter, which still fights on Seheron.

See also: Par Vollen, Minrathous, Circle of Magi, Exalted Marches, Llomerryn Accords, Seheron, Saarebas, Raiders of the Waking Sea, Antiva

### Llomerryn Accords
*event* · PDF p. 165 · `llomerryn-accords`

Cease-fire signed on neutral Llomerryn off Rivain in 7:84 Storm. Tevinter's ambassadors refused to attend or sign.

See also: The Qunari Wars, Rivain and the Rivaini, Tevinter Imperium

### Seheron
*place* · PDF p. 165 · `seheron`

Island a day's sail from Minrathous that the beresaad conquered in three years during the Blessed Age. The war there still rages.

See also: The Qunari Wars, Minrathous

### Raiders of the Waking Sea
*faction* · PDF p. 197 · `raiders-of-the-waking-sea`

Llomerryn pirates who united as the Felicisima Armada against the Qunari and stayed together afterward, raiding the eastern coasts.

See also: The Qunari Wars, The Waking Sea

### Nevarra vs. Orlais
*event* · PDF p. 197 · `nevarra-vs-orlais`

Frequent border skirmishes. Nevarra threw off Orlesian rule in 3:65 Towers, King Tylus held off Orlais in the Exalted Age, and they clashed over the Blasted Hills in 8:70 Blessed. Open war might have to be set off by the player characters.

See also: Nevarra, Orlais, Perendale, The Chevaliers
